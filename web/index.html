MODEL NOTES

A working foundation for a structural digital twin.

The model is a 24 × 4 m, 21-member cross-braced steel truss with a pin at node 1 and a vertical roller at node 5. Three downward 75 kN loads act at the internal bottom nodes. Linear elastic axial members use E = 200 GPa, A = 0.003 m², and ρ = 7850 kg/m³. Added nodal masses and lumped member mass define the dynamic system.

Supports & reaction calculations

Locked translations match the solver restraints. The truss models have translational degrees of freedom only; listed free axes remain unrestrained.

Planar inference research: Damage reduces member elastic modulus. Multiple damaged members can be configured independently. Three independent load cases combine distributed vertical, asymmetric upper vertical, and upper lateral forces. The same physical sensors are read after each load settles. Combined healthy sensitivity reaches rank 21/21, compared with 17/21 for LC1 alone; full rank is a local result, not a global uniqueness or noise guarantee. The random forest uses normalized quasi-static strain and displacement; sensor masks are part of the input. The acceleration panel is a separate simulated dynamic response. The legacy physics fit adjusts all member losses with an ML prior. The noise-aware sparse fit compares healthy, one-member and two-member hypotheses, profiles a common normalized bias, and uses a validation-selected complexity penalty with the configured noise standard deviation. Pair search uses the six best single-member fits. This is not a physics-informed neural network. More than two damaged members and non-common sensor drift are outside this sparse method’s validated family.

The stiffness index averages configured member stiffness retention. It is not remaining capacity or a safety rating. Compression buckling, plasticity, fatigue, joint flexibility, geometric nonlinearity, and environmental changes are excluded. Sparse observations may not identify unique damage states.
